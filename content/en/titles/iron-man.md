---
title: Iron Man
oneLine: A weapons manufacturer builds a flying suit of armor to escape his
  captors, then turns it against the weapons his own company sold.
tagline: The film that started all of this
---

Tony Stark inherited a weapons company and never questioned it. He is brilliant,
rich, and completely uninterested in what happens after a shipment leaves the
factory. That ends when he is ambushed during a demonstration in Afghanistan,
wounded by one of his own devices, and held in a cave by the people who bought it.

He builds two things while he is down there. The first keeps him alive. The
second gets him out.

What makes the film work is that the suit is the smaller half of the story. Stark
comes home and shuts down the division that made him famous, which is a far
stranger decision for the character than learning to fly, and the rest of the
film is about everyone around him refusing to accept it.

If you are starting from nothing, start here. Not because the story demands it,
but because this is where the shared universe learns what it is going to be.

:::detail{title="Where it sits in the timeline"}
This is the first place the project's two dates pull apart, and it is worth
understanding early because it happens repeatedly.

The film was released in May 2008. Marvel's official timeline places the story
itself in 2010, two years later, and later material has been built on that
placement rather than on the release year.

You will see both numbers used elsewhere, often without explanation. Phase Zero
records the release date as a fact about the film and the setting as a fact about
the story, and keeps them in separate fields, because collapsing them is how most
Marvel timelines end up quietly wrong.
:::

:::spoiler{level="minor" from="iron-man"}
After the credits, Nick Fury is waiting in Stark's house to talk about something
called the Avengers Initiative. It is roughly thirty seconds long, it was not
part of the original plan for the film, and it is the moment the shared universe
becomes a stated intention rather than a possibility.
:::
