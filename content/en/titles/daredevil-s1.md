It is a two-hander. Matt Murdock's early nights in a black mask are cut against
Wilson Fisk assembling the money, the police and the buildings, and the season
spends as much time on why Fisk believes he is saving the city as on why
Murdock cannot stop.

It is also the first thing in this material to be genuinely violent in a way
that costs something. Fights end with people unable to stand, including the
protagonist, and the show is patient enough to make that read as damage rather
than as choreography.

:::detail{title="How this became part of the record"}
For years it was an open question whether the Netflix series belonged to the
same continuity as the films. The people who made them always said so; Marvel
Studios did not.

The answer arrived as a filing rather than a statement. When *Echo* was
released in January 2024 the Netflix series were retroactively added to the
official Disney+ timeline, *Daredevil* placed between the *Guardians* films and
*Avengers: Age of Ultron*. Around the same time Marvel Studios' head of
streaming said he felt confident saying *Daredevil* specifically was part of
the Sacred Timeline, which is a carefully bounded sentence and was reported as
one.
:::
