---
title: "Spider-Man: No Way Home"
oneLine: A spell meant to make the world forget who Spider-Man is tears a hole
  in reality instead, and other versions of his story start arriving through it.
tagline: The film that made the multiverse a promise the studio had to keep
---

Peter Parker's identity is public, his life is ruined, and his solution is to
ask a wizard to undo it. The spell goes wrong in the specific way spells go
wrong when the person casting them keeps changing the requirements halfway
through, and what comes through the hole are people who knew a Peter Parker
that was not this one.

What makes it work is that the arrivals are not a victory lap. They are people
carrying decades of unfinished business, and the film is honest that meeting
them costs Peter something rather than giving him something.

:::detail{title="Why this one matters structurally"}
Three studios' worth of Spider-Man films had run in parallel for twenty years
with no connection between them. This is the film that declared them the same
story told in different places, which retroactively changed what every earlier
Spider-Man film was.

It is also the clearest example of the thing this guide keeps insisting on:
watching in release order means the arrivals land as surprises. In story order
they are footnotes.
:::
