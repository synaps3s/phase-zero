Peter Parker is bitten by a radioactive spider in 2015 and starts stopping bike
thefts in Queens, and the story stays that size for as long as it can. That is
the design of the character in this continuity. He is a local hero in a
franchise about global ones, and every time he gets promoted the films are
careful to show what it costs him.

The version of him introduced here is unusually young and unusually
supervised. Tony Stark recruits him out of high school for a fight between
adults, gives him equipment he did not build, and then spends two films
withholding the approval he keeps asking for. That relationship is the spine of the first half
of his arc, and it is also, on rewatch, quite hard to defend.

What he has instead of a philosophy is a habit. He goes back. He goes back into
the building, back after the man who has just tried to kill him, back for the
people who ruined his life, and the films treat that reflex as the actual
power, which is why the suit keeps getting taken off him and it keeps not
mattering.

:::detail{title="The origin you never see"}
This Spider-Man arrives fully formed. There is no radioactive spider on screen,
no wrestling match, no death of Uncle Ben. Ben has already died when we meet
Peter, and May is raising him alone. The films refer to it and never dramatise
it.

That decision is why the character can be introduced in the middle of somebody
else's film, and it is also why his motivation has to be carried entirely by
performance rather than by a scene everyone in the audience already knew was
coming.
:::

:::spoiler{level="major" from="spider-man-no-way-home"}
The spell that was supposed to hide his identity ends up erasing him instead.
Everyone who ever knew Peter Parker forgets that he exists, including MJ and
Ned, and he lets it happen because there is no version of the fix that keeps
them. May is already dead by then.
:::
