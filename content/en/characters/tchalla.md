T'Challa is introduced in someone else's film, at a conference table in Vienna,
sitting beside his father. By the end of the scene his father is dead and he is
the heir to the most technologically advanced country on the planet, which
almost nobody knows exists.

His own film is about the price of that secret. Wakanda has vibranium, medicine
that works, and a border nobody can cross, and it has spent centuries watching
the rest of the continent be taken apart while telling itself that hiding is
the same as safety. The film gives the counter-argument to Erik Stevens, makes
it genuinely strong, and then has T'Challa lose to him.

What follows is the rare thing in this material: a hero who concedes. He wins
the throne back and then does what his opponent wanted, more slowly and without
the killing. His last act in his own film is to establish an outreach centre in
the building where his uncle N'Jobu died, run by Nakia and Shuri, and then to
stand up at the United Nations and tell the world what Wakanda actually is.

:::detail{title="The mantle is a job, not a person"}
Black Panther is a title. It is held by the ruler of Wakanda, it comes with the
heart-shaped herb and the blessing of the panther goddess Bast, and it can be
contested. At T'Challa's coronation, M'Baku of the Jabari challenges him in
ritual combat and very nearly wins.

That distinction matters more than it first appears. It means the story can
continue without the person, which is what eventually happens.
:::

:::spoiler{level="major" from="black-panther-wakanda-forever"}
T'Challa dies before the sequel begins, of an unspecified illness that Shuri
believed the heart-shaped herb could have cured. He had a son with Nakia,
raised in Haiti and named Toussaint, whose Wakandan name is T'Challa. Shuri
takes the mantle.

Chadwick Boseman died of colon cancer in August 2020. Kevin Feige confirmed
that the role would not be recast and that no digital double would be used,
saying Boseman's performance had transcended any previous version of the
character.
:::
