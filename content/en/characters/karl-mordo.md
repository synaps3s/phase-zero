Mordo is the film's best structural idea and the one it does least with. He is
the true believer: rigid, decent, and certain that the rules exist because
breaking them costs something. Then he learns that the Ancient One broke the
central rule for centuries, and that Strange broke time itself to win, and he
concludes that the problem is not the rule-breaking but the number of sorcerers.

He leaves. His last scene sets up a version of him that has not arrived: the man
who decides the world has too many people with power, and starts reducing the
number.
