Bucky Barnes is the only person in this story whose entire arc is about not
being responsible for what he did. Everything Hydra used him for happened; the
question the films keep circling is whether any of it was him.

He starts as the confident one. In 1943 he is a sergeant with a unit and a
future, and Steve Rogers is a small man being told no by every recruiter in New
York. Their positions reverse, and then keep reversing. The fall from the train
should have killed him. The serum he was given without asking is why it did
not.

What follows is seventy years as an instrument: tortured, wiped, kept frozen
between assignments, brought out when Hydra needed somebody dead. When the
films finally give him his memory back, they do not give him relief with it.
They give him the list.

:::detail{title="The trigger words"}
The programming is not portrayed as a mood or a corruption. It is a phrase, and
anybody holding the book it is written in can read it aloud and take the man
out of his own body.

Helmut Zemo does exactly that in *Captain America: Civil War*, in a room, with
a piece of paper, and it is the most frightening thing anyone does in that film
precisely because it takes no power at all.
:::
