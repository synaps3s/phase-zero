Shuri is the best argument the Wakanda films make. The country's advantage is
not the vibranium, which anyone can dig up, but the fact that it has been
allowed to do a century of uninterrupted science with it, and Shuri is what
that looks like in a person.

She is also written as a younger sibling rather than as a prodigy, which is
rarer and much better. She takes the work seriously and her brother not at all,
and the film gets most of its warmth from the gap between those two things.

The Avengers come to her because there is nobody else. The most advanced
technology on Earth belongs to a teenager in a country that officially does not
have any.

:::detail{title="A specific inspiration"}
Reference material notes that the character, as well as being adapted from the
comics, is modelled on Q from the James Bond films: the person in the basement
who hands the hero their equipment and is visibly cleverer than they are.

That reading holds through her first two films and then stops holding, because
Q never has to take the job upstairs.
:::
