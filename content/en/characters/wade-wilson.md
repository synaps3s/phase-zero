Wade Wilson is dishonourably discharged from the Canadian special forces, works
as a freelance mercenary, meets Vanessa, proposes, and is diagnosed with
terminal cancer, roughly in that order. The man who offers him a cure is
running a programme that tortures people until their latent mutant genes
activate, and it works on Wade in the worst possible way: it takes the cancer
and leaves the scarring, permanently.

What separates him from everyone else in this catalogue is that the healing
factor is not presented as a gift. It is the reason he cannot die when he wants
to, the reason grief has nowhere to go, and the joke and the wound in the same
place. The films are extremely funny and they are, structurally, about a man
who has been denied an ending.

He also talks to you. He knows the budget, he knows which actors were available,
he knows what studio owns him. This is the one character here whose continuity
errors are jokes he makes on purpose, which is a useful thing to remember when
you are trying to fit him into a timeline.

:::detail{title="Where this sits, and the version nobody counts"}
These films belong to the X-Men continuity, not the connected Marvel Cinematic
Universe, which is why this catalogue files Wade Wilson under a different
universe designation. That is a production fact rather than a story one, and
the boundary has since been crossed in both directions.

Ryan Reynolds had already played the character once, in
*X-Men Origins: Wolverine* in 2009, a version with his mouth sewn shut that the
2016 film disowns on screen. Wikipedia's article on the series describes
Reynolds in *Deadpool* as reprising that role, which is technically true and
which the film itself spends a scene refusing to accept.
:::
