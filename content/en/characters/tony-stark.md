---
title: Tony Stark
oneLine: A billionaire engineer who builds a powered suit of armor and, over
  roughly a decade, turns from the man who armed the world into the one who
  pays for it.
tagline: Iron Man
---

Tony Stark has no powers. This is the whole point of him. Everything he can do,
he built, and everything he built he can be argued out of or blamed for.

He is the son of Howard Stark, an industrialist who helped shape the century and
was never especially interested in being a father. Tony inherits the company at
twenty-one and runs it the way a prodigy runs anything he was handed: extremely
well, and without ever asking whether it should exist.

The armor changes what he can do. Being held in a cave by people using his own
products changes what he wants, and the distance between those two things is the
character. He spends the rest of his arc trying to make the world safe by
building something, discovering that the something has made things worse, and
building again.

:::detail{title="Reading the character across the films"}
Stark is unusual in this material because his position reverses. He begins as
the argument for individual power without oversight and ends as the loudest voice
for accountability, and the films do not treat either position as obviously
correct.

If you are watching for the first time, the useful thing to track is not the
suits. It is who he is willing to be responsible for, and how that list grows.
:::
