Bruce Banner is a renowned physicist with seven doctorates who spends most of
his screen time trying not to be in the room. He agreed to a Culver University
experiment that Thaddeus Ross described as work on gamma immunity, and which
was in fact an attempt to rebuild the super soldier programme. It went wrong in
the way that defines the rest of his life.

The interesting choice the films make is that nobody ever cures him. He tries,
early and seriously, working with a stranger he only knows as Mr. Blue, and
after that the story stops treating the Hulk as an illness and starts treating
him as a roommate. Banner learns yoga to keep the Hulk down, then spends
years failing to keep him down, and eventually sits in a lab in Mexico that
Stark built for him and negotiates a merger instead.

That is why his arc reads differently from everyone else's. Steve Rogers has to
decide what to do. Tony Stark has to decide what to build. Banner has to decide
how much of himself he is willing to give up in order to be useful, and the
answer changes every film.

:::detail{title="Two actors, one character"}
Edward Norton plays Banner in *The Incredible Hulk*. Mark Ruffalo has played
him in everything since. Kevin Feige has said the studio chose not to bring
Norton back; Norton has said the decision was his, because he did not want to
be tied to a single character for years.

The recasting is also why *The Incredible Hulk* is the entry people most often
forget belongs to this continuity at all. The other reason is contractual:
Universal holds distribution rights to standalone Hulk films, which is a large
part of why the Hulk keeps turning up in other people's films and never again
in one of his own.
:::

:::spoiler{level="major" from="avengers-endgame"}
The gauntlet is his. He is the one who puts it on and reverses the Blip, and it
costs him the use of his right arm. He also admits, afterwards, that he tried
to use the Stones to bring Natasha Romanoff back and that it did not work.
:::
