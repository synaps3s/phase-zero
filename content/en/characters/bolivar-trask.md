Bolivar Trask is the man who invents the Sentinels, the robots that hunt
mutants in the Fox X-Men films. Peter Dinklage plays him in *X-Men: Days of
Future Past*, as a scientist who is entirely sure his work is good for the
world, which Dinklage described as the core of him: drive, blinded by
arrogance.

The film turns on him. In its dark future of 2023, the Sentinels have nearly
wiped out mutants and the people who protect them, and Xavier traces it back to
a single day in 1973, when Mystique assassinated Trask. His death got the
program approved, and her capture gave it her DNA, which is what let the
Sentinels adapt to any mutant power. So Wolverine is sent back to 1973 to
stop the killing, not to stop Trask.

In 1973, Mystique has found out that Trask has been experimenting on mutants,
and goes after him at the Paris Peace Accords. The attempt fails, mutants are
publicly exposed, and Trask uses exactly that to get
President Nixon to authorise the Sentinels.

:::spoiler{title="How it ends"}
At the unveiling in Washington, Mystique gets her chance again, and Xavier
talks her out of it. The country sees a mutant save the President, the
Sentinel program is shut down, and the dark future never happens. Trask is
later arrested for selling military secrets to foreign governments.
:::

:::detail{title="An earlier Trask"}
*X-Men: The Last Stand* has a character called only Trask, the head of the
Department of Homeland Security, played by Bill Duke. Wikipedia's article on
that film calls him probably related to the comic books' Bolivar Trask, but
notes that the film never gives his first name. Wikipedia's list of X-Men film
characters, read the same day, marks Trask as only mentioned in that film. This
entry follows the narrower reading and keeps *Days of Future Past* as his only
appearance, but the two pages do not agree.
:::
