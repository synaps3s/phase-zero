Loki is the second son of Odin and the only one who knew, growing up, that
something was wrong. He is not Asgardian. He was left to die on Jotunheim, the
infant son of the Frost Giant king, and Odin took him home and changed his skin
with magic without ever telling him. He finds out in the middle of an argument,
which is roughly the worst possible way.

Everything he does for the next several films is an attempt to prove that the
version of himself he was handed is a lie, and he does it by being exactly the
person Asgard already suspected he was. He is a liar in a family of warriors,
which he understands as an insult and the story understands as a job.

What keeps him from being simply a villain is that his methods keep failing on
their own terms. He takes the throne twice and does nothing with it. He
conquers a city and loses it in an afternoon. The character only becomes
interesting once he is finally shown the whole record of his own life and made
to sit through it.

:::detail{title="D. B. Cooper"}
Among the things attributed to Loki before the films begin is the D. B. Cooper
hijacking. On 24 November 1971 a man travelling under the name Dan Cooper took
Northwest Orient Airlines Flight 305, a Boeing 727 flying Portland to Seattle,
told the crew he had a bomb, collected two hundred thousand dollars and four
parachutes, and jumped out of the aircraft over southwest Washington. He was
never identified and never found.

The joke is that this material will take a genuinely unresolved piece of
history and hang a god on it, in the same series that asks to be read as a
coherent record. It is worth knowing which parts of the timeline are load
bearing and which are somebody having fun.
:::

:::spoiler{level="major" from="avengers-infinity-war"}
Thanos kills him in the first ten minutes, by hand, in front of Thor, after
Loki hands over the Tesseract to buy his brother's life and then tries one last
trick.

This is the reason the Loki of the series is the 2012 version and not this one:
that Loki's story is finished, and the show is about a different copy of him
who never lived through any of it.
:::
