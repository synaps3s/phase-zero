Johnny Blaze is a motorcycle stuntman. In 1986, at seventeen, Mephistopheles
offers to cure his father's cancer in exchange for his soul; the next morning
the cancer is gone, and his father dies that day in a stunt accident.
Mephistopheles considers the contract fulfilled. Nicolas Cage plays the adult
Johnny in both films, with Matt Long and then Ionut Cristian Lefter as the
boy who signed.

*Ghost Rider* picks him up as a famous stuntman who survives a jump over a
field of trucks and runs into Roxanne, the girl he left behind, now a
reporter. Mephistopheles comes to collect: he makes Johnny the new Ghost
Rider and offers his soul back if he defeats Blackheart, the Devil's own
son, who is looking for a contract worth a thousand souls. Johnny burns down
to the skeleton, learns the Penance Stare, meets a caretaker who knows more
than he says, is jailed for murders he did not commit, and gets out.

:::spoiler{title="Both endings"}
He beats Blackheart, and when Mephistopheles offers to take the curse back
he declines, so that nobody else can be made to sign: he will use the power
against the demon and against anything that harms the innocent.
Mephistopheles vows to make him pay.

*Spirit of Vengeance* finds him hiding in Eastern Europe to keep the Rider
contained. A monk, Moreau, offers to restore his soul if he finds a boy,
Danny, whom the Devil, now calling himself Roarke, means to take over. At a
monastery Johnny confesses the thing the first film never asked him: the
deal was selfish, because his father had accepted dying and Johnny could
not. The Rider is exorcised and he is human, until Danny gives it back and
lets it stay lit in daylight. He throws Roarke back to Hell with the chain,
and the spirit inside him, Zarathos, comes back to itself. The flames turn
blue.
:::
