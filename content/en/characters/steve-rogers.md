Steve Rogers was born in Brooklyn on 4 July 1918. His father was killed by
mustard gas in the First World War, his mother died of tuberculosis, and by
eighteen he was on his own, asthmatic, arrhythmic, partially deaf and too small
for any recruiting office in the country. He kept applying anyway. That is the
part the serum did not give him.

Abraham Erskine chose him for exactly that reason, and the choice is the whole
argument of the character. The programme wanted a weapon and Erskine wanted a
person who would still be recognisable afterwards. What Rogers becomes is not a
stronger soldier so much as a fixed point, and the films keep putting him next
to institutions to see which one bends.

He loses the war he was made for. Seventy years in the ice take his friends,
his century and the woman he was going to have a life with, and he comes back
to a world that has kept his name on a museum wall and forgotten what he was
for. The rest of his story is him finding out, one organisation at a time, that
being on the right side and taking orders are not the same job.

:::detail{title="Why Erskine warns him first"}
The night before the procedure, Erskine tells Rogers about Johann Schmidt, the
Nazi officer who runs Hydra's science division and who took an imperfect early
version of the same treatment. Schmidt was permanently changed by it. Rogers
agrees anyway.

It is a tidy piece of writing, because it sets up a control case. The formula
is the same on both men and the results are not, which means the story can
never credit the chemistry for the character.
:::

:::spoiler{level="major" from="avengers-endgame"}
He survives the final battle. Sent back to return the Infinity Stones to the
moments they were taken from, he stays, marries Peggy Carter, and lives the
life he was cheated out of. He returns as an old man and hands the shield and
the name to Sam Wilson.

How he gets back is genuinely unsettled. Directors Joe and Anthony Russo have
said he created a separate timeline and then found his way home from it. The
writers, Christopher Markus and Stephen McFeely, have said he broke the film's
own time travel rules and lived those decades inside the main timeline, which
would mean two Steve Rogers were present at Peggy Carter's funeral in
*Captain America: Civil War*.
:::
