MJ is Zendaya's character in the Spider-Man films, and she is not Mary Jane
Watson. She is an original character, written as a nod to the comics' Mary
Jane: her name is Michelle Jones, the initials are the joke, and it took the
films three entries to admit that her full surname is Jones-Watson, in a line
where she also says she goes by Michelle Jones. Zendaya was clear about it from
the start, saying she was "100% Michelle" while the internet insisted otherwise.

In *Homecoming* she is the smart, snarky classmate at the back of the room. In
*Far From Home* she is the girl Peter is trying to tell, who has
already worked it out. In *No Way Home* she is in the fight. Zendaya won the
Saturn Award for Best Supporting Actress for the second of those, and the
reviews across all three have been positive in a way the films' supporting cast
does not always get.

:::spoiler{title="After the spell"}
*No Way Home* ends with a spell that erases the world's memory of Peter Parker,
and her with it. *Brand New Day* finds her four years on, graduated from MIT,
sharing an apartment in New York with Ned, and with a new boyfriend. Peter turns
up at the housewarming pretending to be a neighbour, and leaves when he sees
her with someone else.

Then Jean Grey, a telepath at war with Peter, takes control of MJ's mind and
walks her off a rooftop. Peter catches her, hides her at Frank Castle's, and
tells her what she has forgotten. She helps him build two power inhibitors,
finds the letter he wrote her and Ned explaining the spell, and confronts him.
He tells her he loves her. She says she is sorry, that she really has no memory
of him, and that she cannot return it. The film ends with her on the roof of
their old school, turning over the black dahlia necklace he gave her, and does
not tell you what she decides.
:::

:::detail{title="The casting"}
When Zendaya, an African American actress, was cast, speculation that she would
be playing Mary Jane Watson produced a wave of complaint. James Gunn and Mary
Jane's co-creator Stan Lee both defended the casting publicly. Zendaya's own
answer, to The Hollywood Reporter, was that the people upset by it were "just
going to have to get over it", and the films then made the whole argument moot by
giving her a different character. Zendaya has said she worked with the writers
on *Brand New Day* to keep the character's "quirks" after an early draft made
her too "normal".
:::
