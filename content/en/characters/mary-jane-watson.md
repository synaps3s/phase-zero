Mary Jane in these films is written from the outside, as the thing Peter wants,
which is both the trilogy's weakest habit and something the second film
partially fixes. Kirsten Dunst plays her with more impatience than the script
gives her, and the scenes where she asks Peter to simply say something plainly
are the ones that hold up.

The home she comes from is drawn quickly and clearly: a father who shouts
through a wall that Peter can hear from his own room.
