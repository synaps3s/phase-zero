Gamora is introduced as the most dangerous person in the room and spends the
film failing to convince anyone she has changed sides. The problem is not that
she is lying. It is that she was raised in public as Thanos' favourite, and the
galaxy has receipts.

Her origin is one line of dialogue that the films then take completely
seriously: Thanos killed half of the population of her world, including her
mother, and adopted her out of the survivors. Everything she is good at, he
taught her. The films are careful never to describe this as a bond.

The relationship the run actually invests in is with Nebula. They were raised as
competitors by a man who rebuilt the loser after every fight, and getting them
to a place where they can sit in the same room takes four films.

:::detail{title="Which Gamora"}
The Gamora who dies on Vormir in *Avengers: Infinity War* does not come back.
The one who appears in *Avengers: Endgame* and afterwards is a version pulled
out of 2014 by time travel, who has not met Peter Quill, has not left Thanos,
and owes none of these people anything.

The reference article structures itself around that split, giving separate first
and last appearances for the original and the alternate. This catalogue keeps
them in one entry because the article counts one filmography, but the boundary
is clean if it ever needs separating: original from
*Guardians of the Galaxy* to *Avengers: Infinity War*, alternate from
*Avengers: Endgame* onward.
:::
