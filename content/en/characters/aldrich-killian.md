Aldrich Killian co-developed Extremis, the virus that rewrites a body, and
founded Advanced Idea Mechanics to sell what it could do. Guy Pearce plays him
in two registers: the sickly man Tony Stark brushed off years before, and the
polished one who comes back cured, with a private army of Extremis-enhanced
soldiers and a plan to ruin Stark.

The plan is the Mandarin. Killian hires an actor to front a series of terrorist
broadcasts, so the world has a face to fear while A.I.M.'s failed Extremis
experiments, which tend to explode, get blamed on him. Maya Hansen's research is
the science; Killian is the one who turned it into a business.

:::spoiler{title="How it ends"}
He is killed by Pepper Potts, who has been given Extremis herself by then and
turns out to be better at it than he is.
:::

:::detail{title="The twist, and what the series did about it"}
The reveal that the Mandarin was an actor, and that Killian was the real one,
was the most argued-about decision in the film. Many comic readers objected to
what it did to a major villain; others defended it for the social commentary and
for sidestepping the racist caricature of the comics' Mandarin. The series
answered eight years later in *Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings*,
which introduced the actual leader of the Ten Rings, Xu Wenwu, and the actor,
Trevor Slattery, who had been playing his name.
:::
