Sam Wilson's job before any of this was talking to soldiers about what happened
to them. He was a pararescueman who tested an experimental winged suit and lost
his partner Riley to a rocket-propelled grenade, and by the time Steve Rogers
overtakes him on a running track he has built a life out of helping other
people carry the same thing.

That is why the friendship works and why the eventual handover is not a
formality. Rogers is a soldier who was made. Wilson is a soldier who chose to
stop being one and then chose to start again, which is a harder position to
hold and a much better one to give a shield to.

The films are also frank that the shield is not simply his because it was
handed to him. He gives it to a museum. The government gives it to somebody
else. Getting it back is a separate story with its own argument in it, and the
argument is not about worthiness.

:::detail{title="Redwing"}
The drone is introduced in Lagos as a piece of kit and quietly becomes the
thing that distinguishes him from every other flier in this material. Wilson
does not have powers, so his advantage is that he is always looking at the
fight from two places at once.
:::
