Peter Quill is eight years old in 1988 when his mother dies of cancer, and he
runs out of the hospital straight into a Ravager ship. He spends the next
twenty-six years being told what he is by people who are wrong about it. He is
not a legendary outlaw. He is not really a leader. He is a man carrying a
Walkman with a tape of his mother's songs on it, doing an impression of
somebody he once saw in a film.

What makes the Guardians films work is that this is treated as a real problem
rather than a joke about arrested development. Every mistake in the run is his,
and the biggest one costs the universe half of everything, and the films do not
let him off it.

He is also the only person in this catalogue whose defining relationship is
with the man who kidnapped him. Yondu Udonta had been paid to deliver Quill to
his father, worked out what his father actually wanted him for, and kept the
boy instead. He does not explain this to anyone until it is far too late to
matter.

:::detail{title="The punch"}
On Titan, the plan is working. The gauntlet is coming off. Quill learns that
Gamora is dead, hits Thanos, and the hold breaks.

It is the most consequential character decision in the Infinity Saga and it is
not a failure of courage or of intelligence. It is grief, arriving at the worst
possible moment, in a man who has never once managed to put a feeling down.
:::
