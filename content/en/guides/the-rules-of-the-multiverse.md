The multiverse is the load-bearing idea of everything made since 2021, and it is
explained across several entries rather than in one. Here is what has actually
been established, and where.

## There was one timeline, and it was maintained

For thirteen years this story had a single history. *Loki* reveals that this was
not natural: an organisation outside time had been detecting every branch and
deleting it, which is why there had only ever been one.

That organisation is the Time Variance Authority, and the crucial fact about it
is that the protection was a choice somebody made, not a law of physics.

## It stopped being maintained on purpose

The first season of *Loki* ends with that protection deliberately ended. Every
multiverse story afterwards is downstream of a decision taken in one room by two
people.

## Crossing between universes is possible and expensive

*Spider-Man: No Way Home* establishes that a spell can pull people across, badly.
*Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness* establishes dream-walking, and that
one universe colliding with another destroys both. *Deadpool & Wolverine*
establishes that an organisation exists to manage the consequences.

## What the material does not say

It does not say that every version of everybody exists. It does not say that
anything you can imagine is out there somewhere. And it does not treat the other
universes as interchangeable: the ones we visit are specific places with
designations, and this catalogue records those designations on the entries that
name them.

:::detail{title="Why some entries are flagged and others are not"}
An entry is marked as an alternate universe when its own sources say it happens
somewhere other than the main timeline. That is why *What If...?* carries the
flag and *Eyes of Wakanda*, which is animated too, does not.

The flag follows the source rather than the medium or the studio.
:::
