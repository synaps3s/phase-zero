This site has a small number of conventions and they are used everywhere. Once
you know them you can read any page in about half the time.

## Every page is written for two people at once

The person who has seen nothing and the person who has seen everything are
reading the same page, and the layers exist so that neither has to be sacrificed
for the other.

The **one sentence** at the top is for somebody who knows nothing. No jargon, no
names they have not met. If you read only that, you should come away with a
correct impression rather than a vague one.

The **body** assumes curiosity, not knowledge.

A **collapsed grey block** is depth. Production history, continuity problems,
retcons, a date that contradicts another date. Nothing you need is ever hidden
in one; they are for the reader who came for exactly that.

A **collapsed red block** is a spoiler, and it says which title it spoils. That
is a different promise from the grey ones: it hides what happens, not how deep
the page goes.

## Colour is a classification, not decoration

Every entry takes a colour from what it belongs to. A saga owns a colour and a
franchise owns its own within it, so a list running across the years arrives
already sorted by eye.

One colour means something on its own: amber is always the state of having
watched something.

## The badges on a row

A row in the timeline tells you, in order: the year, whether it is out yet, what
kind of thing it is, how many episodes, how long, which franchise, which Earth if
it is not the usual one, and when the story is set.

A row that says **Series** and then a runtime is telling you the length of one
episode. The hours in the tally use the whole thing.

## Everything has a source, and the exceptions say so

The bottom of every entry lists what it was written from and the day somebody
opened it. Where two sources disagree, both are recorded and the disagreement is
explained in a detail block rather than being settled quietly.

Where a figure could not be sourced, the field is empty and the page says why.
That is deliberate: a visible gap is worth more than a plausible guess.
