---
title: Phase One
oneLine: Five films that were each supposed to work alone, and a sixth that
  proved they had not been.
---

Nobody knew this would work. Marvel Studios was a new production company
financing its own films against its characters, having sold the rights to most
of the ones anybody had heard of, and the plan was to introduce a handful of
second-tier heroes separately and then put them in a room together. There was
no precedent for the last step.

So the first five films are built to survive on their own, and they do. *Iron
Man* is a film about an arms manufacturer. *The Incredible Hulk* is a chase.
*Thor* is a fish out of water comedy. *Captain America: The First Avenger* is a
war film. Each one ends with a scene after the credits that would mean nothing
if you had seen none of the others.

Then *The Avengers* arrives and the scenes stop being an in-joke. The phase's
real achievement is not the crossover itself but the fact that the crossover
was not necessary to enjoy any of the parts.

:::detail{title="What was not available"}
The reason this phase is built out of Iron Man, Thor, Hulk and Captain America
rather than the more famous names is that the more famous names had been
licensed elsewhere. Spider-Man was at Sony. The X-Men and the Fantastic Four
were at Fox.

Almost every structural choice in the first decade of this run follows from
that fact, and most of this catalogue's separate universes exist because of it.
:::
