---
title: Stan Lee's cameos
oneLine: The man who co-created most of these characters spent twenty-five years
  turning up inside the films made from them, in a crowd, behind a counter, or
  on a wall.
tagline: One face, forty-seven times
---

It begins in 1989 as a favour in a television film and ends as an institution.
He is a jury foreman, a hot dog vendor, a librarian who hears nothing, a postman
who cannot pronounce Stark, an astronaut telling his life story to cosmic
observers, and finally a photograph on an advert for a carpet cleaning company.

These appearances cross every line this catalogue draws. They run through Marvel
Studios, the Fox films, both Sony continuities and the older unconnected
adaptations, which is why this set belongs to no single story and carries no
franchise colour of its own.

There is a shape to them. Early on he is an extra who happens to be famous, given
no line and no look at the camera. From 2008 he is written in, given a joke, and
placed where the hero is already standing. From 2017 the films begin treating the
appearances as one continuous character, and after 2018 he stops being a person
in the room at all and becomes a picture in it.

:::detail{title="How these are named"}
Where a source records what the on-screen credit actually said, this catalogue
uses it exactly: Stan the Man in Thor, Waterhose Man in The Last Stand, Man in
Times Square in Spider-Man 3, Willie Lumpkin in Fantastic Four, Himself wherever
he plays himself.

Most cameos were never credited under a role name. For those, the designation
line describes the part as the source describes it, and is not a claim about
what appears in the credits.
:::

:::detail{title="What is not here"}
Two cameos were filmed and cut, so they are not appearances: a police officer who
finds a body in Blade in 1998, and a man watching the news in Kick-Ass in 2010.
Neither has been released.

Several later films refer to him without him appearing. A look-alike was written
into the screenplay of Spider-Man: No Way Home but not filmed, and the film
instead uses his birthday as a taxi number. Guardians of the Galaxy Vol. 3
intended a character to be modelled on him before his death. WandaVision puts his
birth date on a licence plate, Loki puts a face that resembles him in a courtroom
portrait, What If...? mentions the pizza parlour named after him, and Morbius
writes his catchphrase on a wall. Those are tributes rather than cameos, and a
set of appearances should not quietly count them as one.

He also appears in work this catalogue does not cover, including the animated
Big Hero 6 and the short film No Good Deed.
:::
