# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume the person you are replying to is acting in good faith and
knows something you do not.

## Our pledge

We want Phase Zero to be a place where someone who has never opened a pull
request can fix a typo without feeling stupid, and where someone who knows the
material deeply can correct a fact without being told they are gatekeeping.

We pledge to make participation in this project a harassment-free experience for
everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability,
ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of
experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance,
race, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

## What we expect

- Assume good faith. A wrong edit is usually a misunderstanding, not sabotage.
- Correct facts, not people. "This date is wrong, here is the source" is useful.
  "You clearly have not seen this" is not.
- Remember that most contributors are not writing in their first language. Fix
  the sentence, do not mock it.
- Newcomers to the material are the reason this project exists. Questions that
  seem obvious to you are the questions we are here to answer.
- Disagreements about content are settled with sources, not with volume.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks of any kind.
- Sexualized language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention.
- Publishing other people's private information without permission.
- Sustained disruption of discussions or review.
- Any conduct which could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a
  professional setting.

## Scope

This applies in the repository, in issues and pull requests, and anywhere
someone is representing the project.

## Enforcement

Report a problem by opening an issue, or privately to the repository owner
through GitHub. Reports are handled confidentially.

Maintainers may edit, hide, or reject comments, commits, code, issues and pull
requests that violate this Code of Conduct, and will explain why when they do.
Repeated or serious violations lead to a temporary or permanent ban from the
project.
